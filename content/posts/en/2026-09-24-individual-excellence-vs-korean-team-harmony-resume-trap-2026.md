## 1. The Cultural Logic Gap: 'I' vs. 'We'

In Western corporate culture, the resume is a stage for the "Hero’s Journey." You are the protagonist who identified a problem, took initiative, and saved the day. You use words like *spearheaded*, *pioneered*, and *transformed*.

In Korea, however, the corporate structure is a highly synchronized orchestra. If the first violin plays too loud, the whole performance is ruined. When a Korean HR manager reads a resume filled with solo achievements, two red flags immediately pop up:

1.  **The Flight Risk**: "This person is so focused on their own growth that they will leave the moment a better offer comes along."
2.  **The Unmanageable Talent**: "Will this person follow the hierarchy? Will they prioritize the team's KPIs over their own portfolio?"

## 2. The 2026 Shift: From 'Specs' to 'In-seong' (Character)

For decades, getting a job in Korea was about "Specs" (Certificates, English scores, University rankings). But as AI begins to handle the technical heavy lifting, Korean HR managers in 2026 have shifted their focus to **In-seong (인성)**—which translates to character, but specifically refers to your ability to maintain **In-hwa (인화)**—harmony within the group.

If your *Jagisogaeseo* (Personal Statement) doesn't explicitly demonstrate your experience in "sacrificing individual credit for the team’s success," you are effectively invisible.

### Case Study: The "Product Manager" Rejection

## 3. The "Growth Process" Trap

The most misunderstood section of a Korean resume is the **Seong-jang Gwa-jeong (Growth Process)**. Most global talents write about their childhood hobbies or their degree. 

In the Korean context, this section is a psychological test. Recruiters are looking for **"Seong-sil" (성실)**—sincerity and diligence. They want to see how you handled a situation where you had to perform a boring, difficult, or repetitive task for the benefit of a group. If you focus only on your "creative breakthroughs," you miss the chance to prove you have the grit to survive the rigorous Korean work culture.
