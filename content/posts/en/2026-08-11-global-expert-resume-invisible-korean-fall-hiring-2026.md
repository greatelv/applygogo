## 1. The Myth of the "One-Page CV" and the Rise of 'Structural Sincerity'

In the West, the one-page resume is king. It’s concise, punchy, and efficient. However, in the Korean recruitment ecosystem—especially with the updated 2026 AI-ATS (Applicant Tracking Systems)—a one-page CV is often interpreted as a lack of effort or, as we call it, a lack of **'Structural Sincerity' (구조적 성실성).**

Korean firms require a **Gyeongnyeok Kisulseo (경력기술서)**, or a Detailed Experience Statement. While your Western resume says you "Increased sales by 20%," a Korean HR manager wants to see the *methodology* of that increase mapped out in a specific hierarchy.

### Why technical fit matters more than "vibes"
The 2026 AI-ATS models used by major Korean firms are programmed to scan for structural completeness. They don't just look for keywords; they look for the relationship between your "Growth Process," "Motive for Application," and "Technical Competencies." If you submit a standard English CV, the system flags it as "Incomplete Data," and your application is buried.

## 2. 'Global Expert' vs. 'Korean Corporate Logic'

Many foreign applicants believe that being a "Global Expert" means they should highlight how different they are. This is a strategic error. In Korea, being an asset means showing how your global skills solve **local Korean problems.**

Take the keyword **'Seongsil' (성실 - Sincerity/Diligence).** In a Western context, saying you are "hard-working" sounds generic and weak. In Korea, *Seongsil* is a core corporate value. However, you cannot just write the word. You must prove it through "Structural Sincerity"—organizing your education history, certifications, and project timelines in the exact chronological and honorific-correct format that reflects Korean professional etiquette.

### The Honorifics Trap
If you are using Google Translate or basic AI to draft your *Jagisogaeseo* (Self-Introduction Letter), you are likely making fatal errors in **Jondaemal** (honorifics). Using the wrong level of politeness in a resume is the fastest way to signal that you will be a "cultural liability" in the office. Korean recruiters can smell a non-native, machine-translated resume from a mile away, and it signals a lack of respect for the local corporate culture.
