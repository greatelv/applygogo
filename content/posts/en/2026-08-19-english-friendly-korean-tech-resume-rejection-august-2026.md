## 1. The Myth of the "Global" HR Team

The biggest misconception global talent has is that "Global Companies" in Korea have "Global HR Standards." 

While the engineering team might speak English and the CEO might have an MBA from Stanford, the **Screening Layer**—the recruiters and the Applicant Tracking Systems (ATS)—are deeply rooted in Korean corporate traditions. In Korea, recruitment is not just about "Can you do the job?" it is about "Do you fit our 'Gyeol' (texture/vibe)?"

Western resumes emphasize **Individual Heroism**: "I increased revenue by 20%," "I led a team of 10." 
Korean HR looks for **Harmonious Professionalism**: "How did you contribute to the organization's collective stability?" and "Is your professional growth consistent (Seongsil)?"

If your resume lacks the structural 'logic' of a Korean application—specifically the detailed **'Gyeongnyeok Kisulseo' (Professional Experience Description)**—local HR managers will find your one-page CV "insincere" or "lacking depth," regardless of your achievements.

## 2. The Missing Pillar: The 'Gyeongnyeok Kisulseo'

In the West, a resume is a highlight reel. In Korea, it is a legalistic record of your professional journey. Most foreign applicants skip the most critical document: the **Gyeongnyeok Kisulseo**.

Unlike a standard CV, a Gyeongnyeok Kisulseo requires:
*   **Detailed Project Breakdowns**: Not just the result, but your specific role within the hierarchy of the project.
*   **The 'Why' and the 'How'**: Korean recruiters want to see the process of how you overcame obstacles. This is where the concept of *'Seongsil'* (sincerity/diligence) comes in. 
*   **Quantified Loyalty**: In Korea, frequent job-hopping is still a red flag. Your resume must be structured to show "Accumulated Competency" rather than just "Skill Acquisition."

Using Google Translate to turn your English CV into Korean is a recipe for disaster. Honorifics (Jondaemal) errors in a resume suggest a lack of cultural respect, which is an instant "No" from HR, even at tech giants.

## 3. "Individual Heroism" vs. "Organizational Value"

In a Silicon Valley resume, you are the star. In a Korean resume, the **Company** is the star, and you are the ultimate supporting actor who makes the company shine.

When we re-engineer resumes at **ApplyGoGo**, we shift the narrative tone. Instead of saying "I revolutionized the workflow," we adapt it to: "I identified inefficiencies within the team structure and implemented a systematic solution that enhanced collective productivity by 15%." 

This subtle shift from *Hero* to *Value-Adder* is the difference between being ghosted and getting an interview.
