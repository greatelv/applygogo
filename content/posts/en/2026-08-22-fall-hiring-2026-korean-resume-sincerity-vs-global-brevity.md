## 1. The 'Sincerity Gap': Why Brevity is a Red Flag

In New York or London, a two-page resume is a cardinal sin. In Seoul, a one-page resume is often seen as a lack of effort. Korean HR managers are not just looking for your "achievements"; they are looking for your **attitude**.

The Korean concept of *Seong-sil* (성실) translates to sincerity, diligence, and faithfulness. When a recruiter opens a file and sees five bullet points under a major role, they don't see "efficiency." They see a candidate who didn't care enough to explain the *how* and the *why* of their work.

To a Korean recruiter, a detailed resume is the first "work product" you submit. If you are brief with your own career story, they assume you will be brief (and potentially careless) with their internal reports.

## 2. 'Gyeongnyeok-kisulseo' vs. The Western CV

Most foreigners make the mistake of using a standard CV for the **Gyeongnyeok-kisulseo (경력기술서)**, or the Detailed Work Experience Statement. 

In Korea, it is standard to provide a granular breakdown of your projects. This includes:
*   **The specific period of the project.**
*   **Your exact role and the size of the team.**
*   **The specific tools and methodologies used.**
*   **The qualitative "lessons learned" alongside quantitative results.**

If your resume says "Increased sales by 20%," a Korean manager wants to know the cultural context, the obstacles you faced within the hierarchy, and how you collaborated with other departments. Without these details, your 20% looks like an empty number.

## 3. The Narrative Power of the 'Jagisogaeseo'

The **Jagisogaeseo (자기소개서)**, or Personal Statement, is where most global talent fails. Westerners tend to treat this as a Cover Letter—a brief summary of why they want the job. 

In Korea, the 'Jaso-seo' is a psychological profile. Recruiters look for specific sections:
1.  **Growth Process (성장과정):** They want to see how your early life shaped your work ethic.
2.  **Strengths and Weaknesses (성격의 장단점):** They want to see self-awareness and how you mitigate your flaws.
3.  **Motive for Application (지원동기):** This must be deeply researched. "I like Korean culture" is a death sentence. "I analyzed your Q3 fiscal report and noticed a gap in your Southeast Asian logistics" is a winning strategy.

Writing this in English and using Google Translate is the fastest way to a rejection. Korean business language requires specific levels of honorifics (*Jondaemal*). A single error in verb endings can make you sound arrogant or uneducated.
