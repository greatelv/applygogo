## 1. The Missing Link: The 'Gyeongnyeok Kisulseo'

In the West, we are taught the "Golden Rule" of resumes: Keep it to one page, focus on achievements, and use punchy bullet points. In Korea, following this advice is the fastest way to get your application moved to the "Discard" pile.

While your English resume (the "Resume") focuses on the *what*, Korean HR is obsessed with the *how* and the *who*. This is captured in a document called the **Gyeongnyeok Kisulseo (경력기술서)**, or the Professional Experience Statement.

Unlike the Western resume, the *Gyeongnyeok Kisulseo* demands:
*   **Organizational Context**: Who did you report to? How many people were in your sub-unit?
*   **Project Specifics**: What was the budget? What were the exact dates of the project lifecycle?
*   **Role Hierarchy**: Were you the lead, the contributor, or the facilitator? 

Without this document, Korean HR looks at your one-page CV and sees a lack of "substance." They see a "Junior" profile because you haven't provided the bureaucratic proof of your seniority.

## 2. 'Impact-First' vs. 'Process-First'

Western resumes are built on the "Action-Result" framework: *"Increased revenue by 20% by implementing X."*

However, the Korean corporate mindset—steeped in Confucian organizational logic—is **Process-First**. An HR manager at Samsung or Hyundai wants to know if you can fit into their existing machine. They are looking for **Seongsil (성실)**—sincerity and diligence—and a respect for the "Standard Operating Procedure."

When you highlight how you "disrupted" a process, a Korean recruiter hears: *"I am a loose cannon who won't follow our internal protocols."* To them, you aren't a "Senior Leader"; you are a "Cultural Risk." You are filtered out as **"Overqualified but Culturally Junior."**

To command the correct salary and rank, you must re-frame your "impact" as "the successful navigation of organizational complexity." You must prove you understand the hierarchy.

## 3. The October Filter: Why Now is the Time to Pivot

October is the month when Korean HR departments finalize their budgets for the coming year. If you are being low-balled now, it's because the recruiter doesn't have enough "evidence" in your file to justify a Senior-level headcount to their finance department. 

They are looking for a reason to pay you more, but your Western-style resume isn't giving them the ammunition they need. You are leaving money on the table because of a formatting error.
