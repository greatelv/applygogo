## 1. The Great Bifurcation: Why One Page is Zero Value

In London or New York, the "one-page rule" is king. In Seoul, the rules of the game are diametrically opposed. Korean HR managers are trained to look for a dual-document structure.

1.  **The Iryeokseo (이력서):** This is the skeleton. It contains your personal data, every school you attended (often dating back to high school), certifications, and a chronological list of companies. It is a factual ledger.
2.  **The Gyeongnyeok Kisulseo (경력기술서):** This is the muscle. Unlike the "Impact Bullets" of a Western CV, this is a technical, project-based breakdown of your career.

When you merge these into one page, you lose the technical depth Korean recruiters trust. Without the *Gyeongnyeok Kisulseo*, the hiring manager assumes you have something to hide or that you didn't care enough to follow the local "standard." For a Korean conglomerate, "Standard" equals "Reliable."

## 2. The Narrative Trap: "Seongsil" (Sincerity) Over Style

Western resumes focus on "I did X to achieve Y." Korean HR looks for **"Seongsil" (성실)**—a combination of diligence, sincerity, and the ability to integrate into a collective. 

Your "Impact Bullets" need to be restructured into a project-based narrative. For each role, you must define:
*   **The Project Period:** Exact dates are non-negotiable.
*   **Your Specific Role:** Where did you sit in the hierarchy?
*   **The Technical Stack/Methodology:** Detailed down to the specific software or framework.
*   **The Quantitative Result:** But explained within the context of the team’s contribution.

If your resume simply says "Led a team of five to increase revenue," a Korean recruiter will ask, "How exactly? What was the interpersonal friction? What were the specific reporting lines?" They aren't just hiring a "worker"; they are hiring a "member."

## 3. The "Translation" Disaster: More Than Just Words

Many applicants think using Google Translate or ChatGPT to turn their English CV into Korean is enough. It isn’t. 

In Korean recruitment, **honorifics (Jondaemal)** and business terminology are the ultimate gatekeepers. Using the wrong level of formality in your *Jagisogaeseo* (Self-Introduction) or failing to use standard corporate nouns will mark you as a "high-risk" hire. Korean recruiters can smell a non-native, machine-translated resume from a mile away. It signals that you aren't ready for the "Nunchi" (intuitive understanding) required in a Korean office.

Furthermore, the formatting matters. While the world uses PDF, many traditional Korean firms still operate on **HWP (Hangul Word Processor)**. If your layout breaks when converted, your candidacy dies in the trash bin.
