## 1. The Paradox of "Power Verbs": Why 'Spearheaded' is a Red Flag

In Western recruitment, we are taught to use power verbs: *Spearheaded, Disrupted, Oversaw, Revolutionized.* These words signal leadership, individual initiative, and a results-oriented mindset.

However, Korean corporate culture, rooted in **organizational harmony (Inhwa)** and **collective diligence**, views these terms through a different lens. To a seasoned HR manager at a Korean conglomerate (Chaebol), saying you "disrupted a process" can imply that you are a "rogue player" who doesn't respect established systems. Saying you "spearheaded" a project alone might suggest you lack the ability to credit your team—a massive red flag in a culture that prizes *Hyeop-eop* (collaboration).

**The Re-Engineering Secret:**
Instead of focusing solely on "I," your resume must reflect "We." You need to pivot from 'Individualistic Brilliance' to 'Organizational Contribution.' Instead of "Disrupted the supply chain," use phrases that translate to "Identified efficiencies to support the team’s overarching goals." You must prove you are *Seongsil* (sincere/diligent) before you prove you are a genius.

## 2. The 'Jagisogaeseo' (Self-Introduction) Labyrinth

The biggest shock for global talent is the **Jagisogaeseo** (often abbreviated as 'Jasoseo'). Unlike a standard Cover Letter, the Jasoseo is a deeply structured document with specific prompts that feel alien to outsiders.

Common prompts for the 2026 Fall season include:
1.  **Growth Process:** They don't want to know where you grew up; they want to know the *values* your family instilled in you and how you overcame adversity.
2.  **Pros and Cons of Personality:** This is a trap. If you say your weakness is "perfectionism," you’ve already lost. Korean HR looks for self-awareness and a proactive plan to mitigate real weaknesses.
3.  **Motivation for Application:** This must be a love letter to the company’s specific history and future vision, not just why the job is good for your career.

If you are simply translating your LinkedIn profile into Korean, you are missing the **narrative logic** required to pass the AI filters. Korean AI bots are programmed to look for keywords associated with "grit," "loyalty," and "cultural fit"—terms that rarely appear in a Western CV.

## 3. The Technical Pitfalls: From HWP to Honorifics

If you are sending a `.docx` or `.pdf` file to a traditional Korean company, you might already be at a disadvantage. Many older conglomerates still operate heavily on **Hangul (HWP)** files. Furthermore, the formatting expectations are rigid.

- **Chronological Order:** Korean resumes often start from high school graduation, not just university.
- **Honorifics (Jondaemal):** This is where most "Google Translate" resumes fail instantly. Using the wrong level of politeness in your Jasoseo is the quickest way to look unprofessional. It signals that you haven't lived the culture, making you a "high-risk" hire.
