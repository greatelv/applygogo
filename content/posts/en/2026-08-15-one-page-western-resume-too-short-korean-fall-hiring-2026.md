## 1. The Cultural Gap: "Seongsil-seong" (Sincerity) vs. Efficiency

In the West, brevity is the soul of wit. We are taught that if you can't summarize your career in one page, you don't understand your own value. However, the Korean hiring psyche operates on a different fundamental principle: **Seongsil-seong (성실성)**, or Sincerity/Diligence.

When a recruiter at a company like Samsung, Hyundai, or Kakao opens your file, they aren't just looking for your technical skills; they are evaluating your *effort*. A one-page resume suggests you spent 15 minutes updating a template. It suggests you are "blasting" the same document to fifty companies. 

In Korea, the document is the first test of your work ethic. If you won't take the time to write a detailed account of your career for the job application, why would they believe you'll work hard once you're hired?

## 2. The Missing Piece: The 'Gyeongnyeok Kisulseo' (Detailed Work Description)

If the standard Resume (Yiryukseo) is the "What," the **Gyeongnyeok Kisulseo (경력기술서)** is the "How" and the "Why." 

Most foreign applicants stop at bullet points:
*   *“Managed a team of 5 and increased sales by 20%.”*

In the 2026 Korean market, this is insufficient. A Korean recruiter wants a narrative structure that captures the project lifecycle, the specific obstacles faced, the collaborative tools used, and the qualitative reflection on the outcome. 

A proper *Gyeongnyeok Kisulseo* should be 2 to 3 pages long. It requires:
1.  **Project Context**: What was the specific market situation when you started?
2.  **Role Specificity**: Not just your title, but your specific "R&R" (Roles and Responsibilities) within a hierarchy.
3.  **The Process**: How did you handle conflict? How did you adapt to the company’s specific culture?
4.  **Quantifiable Results + Qualitative Learning**: What did this teach you about "Seongsil" (sincerity) and teamwork?

Without this document, you are effectively invisible. You are competing against Korean candidates who are submitting 5-page portfolios and 2,000-character "Jagisogaeseo" (Self-Introductions).

## 3. The 2026 Fall Wave: Why This Year is Different

The 2026 hiring landscape in Korea has shifted. With the stabilization of AI-driven HR screening, recruiters are no longer just looking for keywords; they are using sentiment analysis to find candidates who "fit" the corporate DNA. 

Using Google Translate to turn your English bullet points into Korean is a death sentence. Korean business language uses a specific hierarchy of honorifics (Jondaemal) and professional terminology that translation software simply cannot replicate. A single error in "Haeyoche" versus "Hasipsioche" can make you look unprofessional or, worse, disrespectful.
