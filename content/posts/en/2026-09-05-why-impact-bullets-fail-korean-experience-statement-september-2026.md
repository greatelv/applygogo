## 1. The 'Gyeongnyeok Kisulseo' Gap: Why One Page Isn't Enough

In the West, the one-page resume is king. Brevity is seen as a sign of seniority and respect for the reader's time. In Korea, a one-page resume is often viewed as "empty" or "insincere."

While the *Iryeokseo* (이력서) provides the basic facts, the **Gyeongnyeok Kisulseo (경력기술서)**—the Career Description—is where the real decision is made. Western resumes focus almost exclusively on **Results**. Korean HR managers, however, are obsessed with **Process, Hierarchy, and Context.**

When you write *"Increased revenue by 20%,"* a Korean recruiter wonders:
*   What was your specific role within the organizational hierarchy?
*   Which departments did you collaborate with (and how)?
*   What was the specific approval process or 'reporting line' you followed?

If you don't explain the *how* and the *who*, you sound like a freelancer. In a corporate culture that prizes *Seongsil* (성실 - sincerity/diligence) and organizational harmony, a "lone wolf" high-achiever is seen as a flight risk who will quit the moment they don't get their way.

## 2. The "Sincerity Gap" and the Language of Hierarchy

One of the biggest mistakes global talents make is using a direct translation of their English achievements. English is a language of "I" and "Me." Korean corporate culture is a language of "We" and "The Organization."

When your resume is filled with "I spearheaded," "I revolutionized," and "I transformed," it creates a **Sincerity Gap**. To a Korean recruiter, it sounds arrogant and lacks the humility expected in a "Growth Process" (*Seongjang Gwayeong*).

To bridge this gap, you must re-engineer your narrative:
*   **Don't just say 'Passionate':** Use data to show *Seongsil*. Instead of "Passionate about coding," write about your "Record of zero missed deadlines over a 3-year project cycle despite organizational restructuring."
*   **Contextualize Your Impact:** Instead of "Managed a team of 5," write "Coordinated a cross-functional team of 5 under the Director of Operations to align with Q3 corporate KPIs." 

By showing you understand **where you fit in the machine**, you prove that you won't be a "culture shock" liability.

## 3. The "Flight Risk" Red Flag: Why Over-Qualification Fails

In my years reviewing resumes for Korean conglomerates, I've noticed that many foreigners are rejected not because they lack skills, but because they appear "too good to stay." 

Korean companies invest heavily in training and visa sponsorship. If your resume looks like a collection of disparate "impact moments" without a clear, localized narrative of why you want to build a career *specifically in Korea*, they will assume you are using them as a stepping stone.

Your *Jagisogaeseo* (Self-Introduction) must address:
1.  **Motive for Application:** Why *this* Korean company, not just *any* global company?
2.  **Adaptability:** Examples of how you have navigated hierarchical or rigid structures in the past.
