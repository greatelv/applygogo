## 1. The 'Jeong-seong' Gap: Why 3 Sentences Aren't Enough

In the West, the "Summary" or "Objective" section of a resume is a quick elevator pitch. In Korea, this is replaced by the **Jagisogaeseo (Self-Introduction Letter)**. This isn't just a cover letter; it is a multi-section narrative that requires a level of detail that often makes Westerners uncomfortable.

Korean recruiters look for **'Jeong-seong' (정성)**—a term that translates to 'sincerity' or 'devoted effort.' When you submit a brief, bulleted list of achievements, the recruiter sees someone who didn't take the time to explain *how* they fit into the specific history and culture of the company.

## 2. The Trap of "Robot Translation"

Many candidates realize they need a Korean resume and turn to Google Translate or basic AI tools. This is a fatal error. 

Korean is a language defined by **honorifics and hierarchy**. A resume written in the wrong "level" of politeness—or one that uses awkward, direct translations of English idioms—sounds like a robot to an HR manager. 

For example, a phrase like "I am a self-starter" might translate into something that sounds unnervingly aggressive or grammatically bizarre in a professional Korean context. In Korea, you don't just say you are "passionate." You use terms like **'Seong-sil' (Sincerity/Integrity)** and back it up with a narrative of how you overcame a specific hardship (Guk-bok Gwa-jeong). 

If your resume reads like a literal translation, you are signaling that you cannot communicate effectively with a Korean team, even if the job description says "English speakers welcome."

## 3. Re-Engineering Your Narrative for Haban-gi 2026

To succeed in the upcoming August/September hiring wave, you must move beyond the "Action-Result" bullet point. You need to build a bridge between your global experience and Korean corporate values.

*   **The Growth Process:** Don't just list schools. Explain how your upbringing shaped your work ethic.
*   **The Personality Section:** Highlight harmony (Hwa-hap) and team-first thinking over individual accolades.
*   **The Motivation Section:** You must explain "Why this company?" in a way that shows you've researched their 2025-2026 business goals.
